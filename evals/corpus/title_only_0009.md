# The Green Legacy of Wangari Maathai

The bakery opened at five in the morning, and by six the first batch of sourdough was out of the oven. The baker had been doing this for thirty years and could tell from the sound of the loaf whether it had proved long enough. He was rarely wrong, and when he was, he gave that loaf away.

The garden had been designed by someone who understood colour better than most painters. Nothing bloomed at the same time. The purple alliums gave way to orange lilies, and those to white japonica, so there was always something to look at and nothing ever clashed.

The carpenter's workshop was at the back of a yard that was full of wood offcuts that he was 'going to use one day'. The shavings on the floor were deep enough to muffle footsteps. He worked without music because he said he needed to hear what the wood was telling him.
