# The meaning of the economic concept of allocating resources through voluntary exchange

When a farmer sells apples at a price that covers her costs and a customer pays that price because the apples are worth more to him than the money, both parties walk away better off. This simple insight is the foundation of a vast edifice of economic theory.

Under ideal conditions, the repeated interactions of buyers and sellers produce prices that reflect the true scarcity of goods. But the conditions are rarely ideal, and the question of what happens when information is uneven or competition is weak occupies the majority of modern economic research.
